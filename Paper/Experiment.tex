\section{Experiment}
All following transmission and reflection experiments are done at ambient air in the chamber. The evaluation of the signal with a 'Mertz' phase
correction is done automatically by the system. Furthermore, the spectal range for all measurements is set to: $(1500-15000)\,\text{cm}^{-1}$.
\subsection{Gas absorption}
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{Images/GasAbsorption.png}
    \caption{Normalized transmission of an empty chamber filled with ambient air. The spectral
    ranges for gas absorption are highlighted.}
    \label{fig:GasAbsorption}
\end{figure}
To identifythe spectral ranges where the result is influenced by the gas absorption, a measurement at ambient air with a resolution
of $0.3\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ and $N=10$ scans is performed. 
The measurement at air is normalized. The corresponding reference measurement is done under vacuum.
One expects gas absorption dips at frequencies that are similar to the resonance frequencies of the air molecules.
The wavenumbers of the measured gas absorption is shown in tab\,\ref{tab:gasAbsorption}
\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
        \hline\hline
        $\text{CO}_2$ & $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ $\text{CO}_2$ & $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ & $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ \\\hline
        $2350\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ & $3750\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ & $5320\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ & $7180\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ \\
        \hline\hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Regions of strong gas absorption in fig\,\ref{fig:GasAbsorption}. The measured values agree
    with literature values for $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ \cite{cite:GasAbsorption}.}
    \label{tab:gasAbsorption}
\end{table}
All the measured data agrees with the literature values for the gases $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{CO}_2$.
\subsection{Signal to noise ratio}
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{Images/SignalToNoise.png}
    \caption{Transmission measurements for $N_\text{scan}=10$ and $N_\text{scan}=100$ mirror passes.
    The inset shows the part used for evaluation of the signal to noise ratio (see fig.\,\ref{fig:StNlog})}
    \label{fig:StN}
\end{figure}
To determine the influence of the number of scans $N_\text{scan}$ on the signal to noise ratio, measurements with a resolution
of $4\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ are done at various $N_\text{scan}$. Those are transmission measurements with ambient air.
Between $6000\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ and $7000\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ a region with generally less noise (see fig.\,\ref{fig:StN})
and no gas absorption (see fig.\,\ref{fig:GasAbsorption}) is identified. To quantitatively examine the
behaviour of the signal to noise ratio, the mean value $T_\text{mean}$ is divided by the Standard deviation
$T_\text{std}$ for every transmission measurement. 
Thus the signal to noise ratio $SNR$ is given by:
\begin{align}
    SNR = \frac{T_\text{mean}}{T_\text{std}}
\end{align}
Theoretically, one expects $SNR$ to grow according to:
\begin{align}
    SNR = N^{a}
\end{align}
with $a=0.5$ \cite{Script}. To check this dependency, the signal
to noise ratio is plottet for various N's in a double logarithmic scale.
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{Images/STNLog.png}
    \caption{$SNR$ over the number of mirror passes in double logarithmic scaling using the specral range between
    $6000\,\text{cm}^{-1}$ and $7000\,\text{cm}^{-1}$.}
    \label{fig:StNlog}
\end{figure}
From the slope of the linear regression $a$ can be determined as:
\begin{align}
    a = 4.967 \cdot 10^{-1}\pm0.025 ????
\end{align} 
which matches with the expectation.
